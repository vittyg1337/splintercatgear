The Splintercat Standard

What worked, what didn’t, and who should skip it.

A recommendation should be traceable. This is the method every future hands-on Splintercat test will follow—and the promise that research will never be disguised as experience.

The core loop

Measure. Use. Stress. Report.

Specific procedures change by category. The obligation to document them does not.

Measure

Record physical dimensions, usable space, weight, capacity, noise, material details, and the conditions that could affect a result.

Use

Assess unpacking, assembly, placement, everyday operation, cleaning, storage, and interaction over a stated period.

Stress

Apply safe, category-appropriate checks for stability, movement, leakage, wear, or failure. No dangerous spectacle.

Report

Show original evidence, strengths, drawbacks, uncertainty, test dates, and the cats or homes for which it is a poor fit.

Before a product enters the lab

We begin with the decision, not the product. What problem is a household trying to solve? Which attributes are meaningful? What does credible welfare or safety guidance say? Which claims can be measured, and which depend on individual preference?

A test plan is written before a verdict. It identifies the intended user, comparison set, conditions, measurements, observation period, safety limits, and known blind spots.

Every hands-on test record will include

  • How the item was acquired: purchased, borrowed, or provided by a manufacturer.
  • The exact model, size, and version tested.
  • Test dates, duration, setting, and relevant household conditions.
  • Applicable cat characteristics without exposing private household information.
  • Measurements, original photographs, observations, failures, and uncertainty.
  • Who may benefit, who should skip it, and what alternatives deserve consideration.

Category-specific methods

A fountain and a cat tree should not be judged by the same checklist. Before the first review in each category, Splintercat will publish a category method defining the tests that matter.

Climbing furniture

Usable platform dimensions, base footprint, assembly, fasteners, anchoring, movement under controlled load, surface wear, and cleaning access.

Water fountains

Usable capacity, sound under defined conditions, access, assembly, disassembly, cleaning burden, replacement parts, and power-loss behaviour.

Litter equipment

Interior dimensions, entry access, tracking, leakage, cleaning, noise where relevant, consumables, and maintenance time.

Carriers

Opening access, structure, closure security, usable interior space, handling, vehicle fit, cleaning, storage, and manufacturer-rated limits.

Content labels

Every substantive page carries one of these labels near the top:

Hands-on tested

Direct evaluation

Physical testing under a published method, with original evidence and stated limits.

Research guide

Evidence synthesis

Credible guidance, manuals, specifications, and cited sources. No rating or test badge.

Field note

Everyday context

A clearly labelled, longer-term observation. Useful, but not universal proof.

Ratings and “best” claims

Splintercat will not launch a numerical score merely to make a page look complete. A category receives ratings only after a consistent method and a meaningful comparison set exist. The page will explain what the score represents.

“Best” requires a defined use case. A stable tower for a large, active cat in a permanent room is a different decision from a compact scratcher for a renter with limited floor space.

Money and editorial control

Manufacturers cannot pay for a ranking or edit a verdict. Sponsorship, samples, discounts, affiliate relationships, and other material connections will be disclosed beside the relevant content. Affiliate compensation may affect which retailer links are offered; it does not change the published testing method.

See the affiliate disclosure and editorial policy for the operating rules.

Updates, failures, and corrections

Products change. Prices, availability, specifications, and long-term performance change too. Material updates will carry a real revision date and a note explaining what changed. If an error affects a conclusion, the correction will be visible rather than silently buried.

The current launch state

The lab is being built. Pages marked Hands-on Tested will not appear until an actual test record exists. The site’s first published resource is therefore labelled honestly as a Research Guide.
